the field starts bare — a pin is an obstacle no filter has in its map, so pieces are a thing you ask for. Load layout puts out Figure FO-2, the Override starting set: 36 cups and 37 pins, the other 20 cups and 26 pins being match loads and preloads. A reset restores it, so two runs face the same field. Pins and cups are rigid bodies in Rapier: they roll, spin, knock each other along, and gather in front of a plough. Drag a piece to move it. The localization beams do not stop on them; the claw's own sensor does. The claw takes whatever is within 4.5 in of the jaws when it closes, and nests it in a goal — or in a cup — if it opens against one.

Other robots partners and opponents

press path, then click the field to lay points — right-click takes one back, Esc finishes and starts it driving. Waypoints are numbered and can be dragged afterwards. Drag the robot itself to place it by hand, which stops it driving; alt-drag turns it.

Toggles who owns the yellow pins

ram the front of the robot into the middle of a wall and the roller turns one face. A yellow pin placed in that quadrant is worth 10 to whoever owns the toggle at the end, so a face is a bigger swing than a pin. Click a chip to set one by hand. Height is not modelled — the roller sits on the top rail, and this cannot tell you whether your flipper reaches it.
